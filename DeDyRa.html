Franco Severo (Sorbonne Université)

Weighted isoperimetry implies percolation

Abstract: Given a graph with edge weights $(\lambda_e)$, one can define the associated isoperimetric profile $\phi(n)$ as the infimum over the total weight of the edge boundaries of vertex subsets of size at least $n$. Our main result shows that if $\phi$ grows sufficiently fast (in particular, as any power law), then percolation occurs when each edge is open with probability $p_e=1-e^{-\lambda_e}$. As applications of this result, we prove the truncation conjecture of Sidoravicius, Surgailis and Vares for non-summable long-range percolation on $\mathbb{Z}^d$, as well as a conjecture of Easo and Hutchcroft on the percolation threshold of transitive graphs with large degree.

As I will explain in the talk, the homogeneous case of this result was recently obtained via a standard Peierls argument together with a clever probabilistic method for counting cutsets, which has no chance to work when weights can be arbitrarily small. Our main contribution is to develop a new Peierls argument which takes into account internal and external connectivity costs in addition to the cost of the blocking surface.

Based on joint work with Ivailo Hartarsky and Augusto Teixeira.

20. 11. Matthias Irlbeck University of Hamburg Non-vanishing uniqueness threshold for hyperbolic Poisson-Voronoi percolation in dimension at least three
Abstract

We study the threshold for the existence of exactly one unbounded black cluster in Poisson-Voronoi percolation on $d$-dimensional hyperbolic space, for $d\ge 3$. We start with a Poisson point process of constant intensity $\lambda > 0$ and consider its Voronoi tessellation. We then color each cell black independently with probability $p$. The uniqueness threshold $p_u(\lambda)$ is the infimum over all $p$ in $[0,1]$ for which there exists a unique unbounded black cluster almost surely. We will introduce the model and sketch a proof that, for each fixed dimension $d \ge 3$, this threshold remains bounded away from zero as $\lambda$ tends to zero. This answers a question of Grebík and Recke.
